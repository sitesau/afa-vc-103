Asset Finance Australia Buyer Tips Explained

The foundation of a sound equipment purchase is choosing the correct funding facility. This choice generally boils down to who owns the asset during the term and how you want it treated for accounting purposes. The parent guide to asset finance australia details these structures, but they broadly fall into three categories. A chattel mortgage involves borrowing to buy the asset, giving you ownership from day one while the lender takes security over it. This suits businesses wanting to claim GST and depreciation upfront. Alternatively, a finance lease means the financier owns the item and leases it to you, offering fixed payments and keeping the asset off your balance sheet. For those needing short-term use of fast-depreciating items like IT hardware, a rental or operating lease allows you to pay for use without ownership obligations.

Decoding the true cost of funding

Businesses often focus solely on the headline interest rate, yet the actual expense depends on several moving parts. Lenders assess risk based on the asset's age and type, with newer mainstream equipment typically attracting sharper rates than specialised or ageing gear. The term length, usually matched to the working life of the asset, also influences the total interest paid. One of the most critical levers is the balloon or residual payment. This is a lump sum due at the end of the term that lowers your monthly repayments but must be refinanced or paid out. Additionally, while many facilities fund 100 percent of the purchase price, contributing a deposit can secure a more favourable rate. Comparing structured quotes is essential, as two businesses buying identical machinery may receive vastly different offers based on their trading history and financials.

Preparing your business for approval

Approval for asset finance is often faster than unsecured lending because the equipment itself acts as security. However, lenders still need assurance that your business can service the debt. Preparation is key to avoiding delays. You should confirm the specific asset, the supplier and the total drive-away or invoice price before approaching lenders. Financial documents such as recent bank statements, tax returns and proof of ABN activity are standard requirements. A strong trading history and clear financials improve your profile. It is also vital to have a clear view of your cash flow, as the repayments should ideally align with the income the asset generates. If you are comparing asset finance australia options, presenting a complete picture of your business health helps brokers secure the most competitive terms.

Identifying financeable assets

Almost any tangible item that can be valued and resold qualifies as an asset for finance purposes. This broad definition covers a vast range of business equipment. Commonly financed items include motor vehicles, utes, trucks and buses for transport, as well as excavators and loaders for the construction sector. Manufacturers often fund CNC machinery and plant equipment, while medical, dental and veterinary practices use these facilities for fit-outs and diagnostic tools. Even commercial kitchen gear, hospitality equipment and IT hardware like servers are regularly financed. The key requirement is that the item is identifiable and holds resale value, allowing the financier to recover costs if necessary. Specialist equipment finance brokers can assist in categorising specific assets for funding applications.

  1. Confirm the asset details. Identify the specific equipment, the supplier and the total invoice or drive-away price required for the purchase.
  2. Decide on ownership. Determine if you need to own the asset for tax purposes, which guides your choice between a chattel mortgage or a lease.
  3. Gather business documentation. Compile your ABN, trading history, bank statements and recent financials to demonstrate serviceability.
  4. Compare structured quotes. Review offers from multiple lenders, looking beyond the interest rate at balloon payments, fees and total cost.
  5. Settle and document. Finalise the agreement, take delivery of the asset and retain all documents for your accountant and Business Activity Statement.

Common questions

What is the difference between a chattel mortgage and a finance lease? The primary difference lies in ownership. With a chattel mortgage, you own the asset immediately and claim depreciation and GST. In a finance lease, the financier owns the asset, you pay rent to use it, and it sits off your balance sheet.

Can I finance the full cost of the equipment? Yes, many asset finance facilities fund the full purchase price, though making a deposit can sometimes help reduce the interest rate offered by the lender.

What assets are eligible for finance? Any tangible and identifiable item with resale value is generally eligible. This includes vehicles, machinery, plant equipment, medical tools, IT hardware and commercial fit-outs.
